THE MEDICAL SET PIECE

A pitchside emergency should be rehearsed before kick-off: clear plan, clear roles, correct positioning, ready equipment and closed-loop communication.

  • The team doctor enters first, followed by the rest of the medical team.
  • Team leader (Black 1) — leads, ideally hands-off, and steps in if a job is not being done.
  • Around the player: Orange 2 (directly behind the head), Red 3 (chest), Blue 4 (pelvis), White 5 (legs) and Green 6 (bag and AED, at the head on the player's left).

BEFORE KICK-OFF: 5 KEY PEAP COMPONENTS

M — Meeting

Who is involved?

  • Home team doctor
  • Visiting team doctor
  • Venue medical officer
  • Pitchside paramedics

C — Contacts

Who do we call and where do we go?

  • Emergency numbers
  • Access / egress gates
  • Ambulance location
  • Receiving hospital

R — Roles & positioning

Who does what and where?

  • Assign the team leader and the five colour roles
  • Brief any extrication team the venue provides
  • Confirm positions
  • Brief the team

E — Equipment

Is everything ready?

  • Emergency care bag
  • AED + pads
  • Oxygen + BVM
  • Scoop / spinal board / basket + straps

T — Training

Have we rehearsed?

  • Rehearse the critical scenarios before the event
  • Sudden cardiac arrest · cervical spine injury · trauma
  • Every colour practises its own role, with the real equipment
  • Debrief, then fix the gaps

M-CRET = Meet → Call → Position → Check → Train

FIFA — before the tournament
The whole medical team trains in the FIFA set piece with the FIFA Match Doctor (usually Black, the team leader) 5–7 days before the first match, on the standard FIFA equipment.
FIFA — on matchday
Between 2 hours and 1 hour before kick-off the team runs moulages (simulations) of the most critical scenarios, led by the FIFA Match Doctor.
FIFA — everywhere
Use and rehearse the PEAP in all football settings — training sessions as well as matches — adapted to the site and the expertise available.
How often
Practise the emergency plan at least once a year with everyone likely to respond, and review it briefly with the home and visiting medical staff before each competition.
How to rehearse
Hands-on and scenario-based, at different places in the venue: sudden cardiac arrest, head and neck injury, heat stroke and limb injuries. Record when, where, who took part and which scenarios.
After a real emergency
Debrief with everyone involved and use it to improve the plan.
Why it matters
Aim for the first shock within 3 minutes of collapse. In one US study, 89% of school athletes survived a cardiac arrest when CPR and defibrillation were prompt — and survival was higher with an AED already on site.

Sources: FIFA Medical Set Piece — Protocols for on-field interventions (2025); consensus on sudden cardiac arrest on the field of play (Br J Sports Med, 2017); NATA position statement on emergency action plans in sport (J Athl Train, 2024).

GOLDEN RULE: NON-CONTACT COLLAPSE

Presume sudden cardiac arrest (SCA) until proven otherwise

ENTER IMMEDIATELY → ASSESS → START CPR → APPLY THE AED WITHIN 30 SECONDS OF IT ARRIVING. Do not delay CPR / defibrillation for C-spine or fracture assessment in a non-contact collapse.

2025 resuscitation guidelines: call at once

The Resuscitation Council UK's 2025 guidelines say to call for help for anyone who is unresponsive, without first waiting to judge their breathing. On the pitch that is the set piece already: the team enters at once, Black makes sure the ambulance is called and Green brings the AED. Red's 10-second check of breathing and pulse decides on compressions — it never holds up the call.

MEDICAL TEAM MEMBERS

RolePositionMain responsibility
Team leader (Black 1)Player's left, level with the chestHands off, in overall control: steps in if a job is not being done. CPR/AED flow, the transfer plan, ambulance and referee liaison.

Six roles
Black, Orange, Red, Green, Blue and White — the colours on FIFA's PEAP poster.
Numbers
Each colour has a number — Black 1, Orange 2, Red 3, Blue 4, White 5, Green 6 — in the order FIFA's Emergency Care Manual lists the team members. When the crowd is too loud to hear, the team leader can call or hand-signal a role by its number.
Orange's position
This guide places Orange directly behind the head, in line with the body, for manual in-line stabilisation. FIFA's poster marks Orange at the head.
Smaller teams
One person can cover more than one role if they are able to. Every role still has to be filled.
Extrication team
The current FIFA PEAP shows six roles: Black, Orange, Red, Green, Blue and White. Earlier versions also marked a separate extrication team in yellow, usually four first aiders provided by the stadium. Some venues and teaching centres still use one. If yours does, include them in the pre-match plan: they wait beside the scoop and basket, facing the player, and join only when the team leader calls them — to help with a log roll or to carry.

FIFA ECM: the Chest member should be one of the most experienced — they lead if no one else can. Pelvis and Legs need no medical skills but must be trained in the log roll. First aiders and paramedics only help if they are part of the plan.

ENTERING THE PITCH (FIFA RULES)

  1. The referee assesses the incident.
  2. If needed, the referee signals the team doctor onto the pitch.
  3. Only then, if needed, the referee calls the medical team — both hands held as if carrying a stretcher.

Exception: non-contact collapse

A player who collapses without contact is treated as sudden cardiac arrest — the medical team runs on without waiting for the referee's signal.

SIDELINE POSITION AND READY POSITION

  • At FIFA matches the medical team sits to the left of Team A's bench, with the extrication kit set up beside the bench.
  • The team leader may call the team into the “ready” position.
  • Ready: move along the touchline to the point closest to the incident and squat in your assigned position with the stretcher.
  • Not needed: return to your seats, passing behind the team benches where possible.
Medical team at FIFA matches
8–9 members: one emergency physician experienced in out-of-hospital care plus paramedics / health professionals doing BLS and ALS, with 2–3 reserves. The same team works every match at a venue.
Training
The whole team trains in the FIFA set piece with the FIFA Match Doctor 5–7 days before the first match, on the standard FIFA equipment.
FIFA Match Doctor
Usually Black, the team leader. Sits with and leads the team, reviews injury mechanisms on a replay device (often with a remote injury spotter), makes their own assessment, calls the ready position, and enters for potentially serious injury or concussion — then directs when the team comes on with the equipment.

MATCHDAY PRINCIPLES

Kick-off −2 h
Team present, equipment checked; player medical centre staffed.
−2 h to −1 h
Moulages (simulations) of the most critical scenarios, led by the FIFA Match Doctor.
Half-time · cool-down
Medical team always present.
Post-match
Medical centre staff stay until the last player has left the stadium.
  • Everyone knows their PEAP colour, wears their bib and shows no team bias.
  • Equipment set up next to the bench; no phones, photos or food pitchside.

PITCHSIDE POSITIONING & TEAM ERGONOMICS

Extrication kit: below the feet, in line with the player. Basket 2–3 basket lengths from the feet; scoop taken out, opened and split, laid between the feet and the basket.

Equipment zone
AED + O2 + emergency care bag at the player's head / left side.
Extrication zone
Below the player's feet, in line with the body: basket 2–3 basket lengths from the feet, scoop opened out and split between the feet and the basket. Kept back until required.
Team leader
Hands-off, with a full view of the whole scene. Keep the working area clean and uncluttered.

EXTRICATION SET PIECE

PREPARE → TILT → SCOOP → STRAP → BASKET → GO

  • Orange (the head) commands a gentle 15–20 degree tilt.
  • Reconnect the scoop head first, then feet.
  • Strap: Chest → Pelvis → Legs → Head.
Medical kit (FIFA)
FIFA bag + oxygen to one side of the head; one person hands out equipment.
Extrication kit (FIFA)
Basket in line with the feet, 2–3 basket lengths away. Scoop out of the basket, extended and split between basket and player.
Extrication team, if the venue has one
Stand or kneel beside the scoop and basket, facing the player, and wait for the team leader. One stays free to pass the scoop and slide the basket in.

The set piece above is for trauma. For a cardiac arrest in progress the player is moved on a long spinal board (a scoop can also be used) with the AED strapped on as one unit — see the cardiac arrest module.

  • FIFA team kit (two identical sets): basket stretcher, split scoop or spine board, head blocks, cervical collar, straps or spider; AED; oxygen bottle; splints; an emergency bag the team knows well.
  • Entering: Green carries the emergency bag; the others carry the basket with the scoop / board, head blocks, collar and straps inside.

CLOSED-LOOP COMMUNICATION

NAME → COMMAND → REPEAT → CONFIRM

Example — Team leader: “Green, apply the AED.” Green: “Applying AED.” Green: “AED attached.”
