CLEARING THE CERVICAL SPINE

FIFA: the spine is cleared only if all 6 are met.

  1. No midline tenderness
  2. Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) 15
  3. No distracting injury
  4. No neurology
  5. No concerning mechanism (e.g. fall onto the head, axial load)
  6. Active range of movement: 45° lateral rotation to each side — the player moves, not the clinician

Workshop mnemonic — NSAID

Neurological deficit · Spinal midline tenderness · Altered mental status (GCS < 15) · Intoxication (includes strong analgesics) · Distracting injury. Any one present → the spine cannot be cleared; extricate in a collar.

  • Start manual in-line stabilisation (MILS) and palpate for midline tenderness.
  • An alert, conscious player splints their own neck — an early collar is not required.
  • Clearing can wait until the primary survey is done. The collar goes on as part of extrication.
  • FOCUS red flags for the neck: midline tenderness, restricted active movement, unexplained limb weakness or sensory change, numbness / tingling.

COLLAR AND HEAD BLOCKS

  1. Maintain MILS throughout.
  2. Size: neutral head, line from the chin back to the sternomastoid, measure fingerbreadths down to trapezius.
  3. Between two sizes → start with the SMALLER one (too big extends the neck).
  4. Position the posterior section; support the chin in the chin piece. Secure without over-tightening.
  5. Check the mouth can still open, then reassess the airway.
  6. Blocks placed evenly, fixed to the device, then tape. Keep access to the airway and ears.

MILS → SIZE → COLLAR → STRAPS → BLOCKS → TAPE → RELEASE

  • Contraindication: airway obstruction — keep MILS instead until the airway is dealt with.
  • A collar alone does not immobilise. Keep MILS until blocks and tape are on.
  • Follow local guidance — some countries no longer use collars.

LOG ROLL AND EXTRICATION

Head (Orange)
MILS. Controls the move: explains the signal and says how far — 90° log roll, or a 15–20° tilt for a scoop.
Chest (Red)
One hand on the far shoulder, one on the hip.
Pelvis (Blue)
One hand on the waist, the other under the knee.
Legs (White)
One hand under the lower leg, the other under the ankle.
Fifth person (Green / First Aiders)
Places the scoop or board.

Extrication order

  1. Size and fit the collar — check the mouth can still open.
  2. Log roll or tilt onto the scoop — Orange leads the count.
  3. Apply straps — the first, centre strap lined up with the sternum.
  4. Apply head blocks and fix them to the device, then tape.
  5. Only now can MILS be released.
  6. Slide the basket under the scoop and extricate with 6 people if possible.

Workshop standard for trauma: scoop stretcher + basket, set up below the feet in line with the player — basket 2–3 lengths away. A scoop moves the player only ~15° instead of a 90° log roll.
