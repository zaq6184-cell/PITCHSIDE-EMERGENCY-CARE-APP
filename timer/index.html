LIGHTNING · THE 30/30 RULE

Count from the flash to the thunder. Under 30 seconds the storm is within about 10 km: stop play. Resume only 30 minutes after the last flash or thunder. Your competition's rules may be stricter.

Tap FLASH the moment you see lightning, then THUNDER when you hear it.

  • Safe shelter: a fully enclosed building with wiring and plumbing, or a hard-topped vehicle with the windows shut. Not safe: dugouts, tents, open stands, under trees, or beside floodlight poles and fences.
  • Someone struck: they carry no charge — go to them at once. Not breathing normally means cardiac arrest: CPR and the AED. With several casualties, treat those who look lifeless first (reverse triage); those who are breathing usually recover.

Sources: NATA position statement on lightning safety for athletics and recreation; Football NSW lightning policy.

CARRY RULES

  • First choice: the ambulance or a buggy comes onto the pitch to the player, with a mechanical CPR device (LUCAS) where available. Carrying is a last resort.
  • Moving the player is considered after the third shock and medications; the team leader decides.
  • Carry only on the team leader's command, and never for more than 10 seconds — count out loud.
  • At “Ten seconds!”: player down, compressions at once.
  • At least 2 minutes of compressions before the next carry. Repeat until the player is inside the ambulance.
