WHAT'S NEW

Version 1.3.0 · October 2026

  • Everything new checked against FIFAEach new card and page was checked line by line against the FIFA Emergency Care Manual. Cardiac arrest in the cold now follows FIFA: adrenaline withheld and shocks delayed until 30 °C. Heat stroke can come with cool, sweaty skin, and is treated as heat stroke when no rectal temperature can be taken. The chest pain, women's health, cold, altitude, eye, nose, tooth, chest, abdomen and pelvis cards gained FIFA's details.
  • Medical emergency cards come to lifeEach medical emergency card now plays a short, silent loop of its key action, made with Higgsfield from its picture and labelled AI-generated. It starts when the card is on screen, stops when it scrolls away, and people who ask for reduced motion or data saving keep the still picture.
  • Medical emergencies moved to More pitchside topicsMedical emergencies now sits on the home page under More pitchside topics, with the Injury map, Heat illness and the new Cold and altitude page. The Tools tab keeps the match-day tools.
  • Chest pain and women's health cardsTwo new medical cards from the FIFA manual: chest pain (aspirin, GTN and the other causes to rule out) and lower abdominal pain or bleeding in a female player.
  • Cold and altitudeFIFA's environmental chapter on one page: S.H.E.L.T.E.R., hypothermia, frostbite, cold urticaria, mountain sickness, HACE and HAPE, with an altitude planner that shows the band, the risk and the days to allow for a staged ascent.
  • Injury map checked against FIFAA new hip and pelvis area; the jaw and cheekbone, flail chest, tension pneumothorax, elbow and collarbone dislocations and the fracture-dislocated ankle; a 30-minute clock for a knocked-out tooth; and the bleeding rule correctly cited as Law 5.
  • Heat illness, aligned with FIFAWBGT measured 90 and 60 minutes before kick-off, ice water at 1–17 °C, a blood glucose check, legs raised for heat exhaustion, and a warning about low sodium.
  • Languages recheckedEvery page was checked again in all five languages: a number and its unit now stay together on one line, and the new pages are translated in full.
  • Medical cards checked against the FIFA manualThe five cards now follow chapters 4 and 5 of the FIFA Emergency Care Manual and its bag list: the 1:10 000 adrenaline warning, inhaler puffs every 60 seconds, glucose rather than sports drinks, midazolam for a long seizure and back blows from behind. The handover can now record glucagon and a benzodiazepine.
  • Pictures for the medical cards and the handoverEach medical emergency card shows its key action, such as the auto-injector into the thigh, the inhaler through a spacer and back blows, and the handover page shows the team leader handing over at the ambulance. AI-generated, and labelled as such.
  • Medical emergency cardsAnaphylaxis, asthma, low blood sugar, seizure and choking: what to recognise, what the team does, the adult doses if trained, and when to call the ambulance, with the points that matter in football.
  • Handover to the ambulance crewAn ATMIST card (Age, Time, Mechanism, Injuries, Signs, Treatment) that fills in from the arrest timer, ready to read out or share. Nothing is saved.

PURPOSE OF THE APP

A free study guide to pitchside emergency care in football, built around FIFA's medical set piece: the six colour roles, sudden cardiac arrest, the primary survey, cervical spine injury, concussion assessment and the emergency care bag — with team drills in 3D and in augmented reality, a case simulator, an AED pads trainer, heat-illness and injury tools, instructor tools and match-day tools. It summarises FIFA's published guidance in plain language and links to FIFA's own documents and films.

It is meant for doctors, paramedics, physiotherapists, first aiders and anyone else who may be part of a pitchside medical team, to learn the roles and rehearse them before they are needed.

PRIVACY

Everything you do in this app is confidential and stays on this device. There is no account and no sign-in, and nothing is sent to the developers or to anyone else: no cookies, no tracking, no adverts.

Kept on this device only

  • Settings: language, dark mode and sound.
  • Progress: badges, best scores and times, and the roles, modules, drills and cases you have opened.
  • What you enter: the arrest timer log, the match plan, the practical scorer's sessions and the lightning countdown.
  • Offline copies: the app's own pages, pictures, sounds and films, saved so it works at the pitch without a signal. They hold nothing about you.

All of it stays in this browser's storage on this device. Something leaves it only when you share it yourself, such as a debrief or a plan, through your phone's share sheet.

Permissions the app may ask for

  • Motion sensorsCPR trainer, counting by motion on a manikinCounts your compressions from the phone's movement. iPhones and iPads ask first. Nothing is recorded.
  • CameraLive AR in Chrome on AndroidPlaces the 3D team on your floor. The picture stays on the phone and nothing is recorded.

Both are optional: say no and everything else still works.

Keeping the screen on (timers and drills), vibration, full screen, sharing, copying and installing the app need no permission.

The app never asks for your microphone, location, contacts, photos or notifications.

When the app goes online

  • Films: YouTube's films play in its privacy-enhanced mode when you tap one, and FIFA's open on FIFA's website. Those sites follow their own privacy policies.
  • Links: FIFA's documents and the medical journals open on their own websites.
  • Live class quiz: phones connect straight to the instructor's screen. Public relays only introduce them, encrypted with the room code. The nickname you type and your answers go only to that screen, for that quiz, and nothing is stored.
  • AR viewers: Google's AR viewer on Android and AR Quick Look on iPhone open the app's own 3D models.

Delete your data

Removes your settings, progress, logs, plans and scorer sessions from this device. The offline copies hold nothing about you and stay, so the app still works without a signal; clear this site's data in your browser's settings to remove those too.
